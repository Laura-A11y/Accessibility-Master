## Free Accessibility Certifications
I took all of these courses myself, so I’d be able to tell you which ones to pick based on your level of web accessibility proficiency and learning goals. All are free, online, and self-paced.

## Easy Introductions for Beginners
These are great as introductory courses for three simple reasons: They are well researched, they emphasize intersectionality, and they are short. There are so many web accessibility courses and certifications out there. It is hard to tell which ones are a good measure of knowledge and skill.
In my opinion, the real danger lies in underestimating how topics overlap and intertwine. Starting off with a good awareness of intersectionality gives you a stronger foundation.
Plus, these are short; maybe two hours each. They can fit easily into your work week or maybe even your lunch break if you are especially ambitious. At the beginning, accessibility, inclusion and everything related to it can seem overwhelming (because it is!). Having a clearly defined awareness training can put structure into your learning journey.

### [UNITAR Inclusive Learning Design](https://learningsolutions.unitar.org/inclusive-learning-design-course) (for educators, workshop planning, or mentoring)
A very short training program, maybe two hours all in all. But the examples are not the usual go-to ones and the practice quizzes are written in a way that makes you pause and think. I also appreciated that this training puts intersectionality front and center.
In my professional network (and likely also yours, dear reader), we often mean specifically disability inclusion when we use the term “inclusion” but effectively it refers to all dimensions that can bar one from equal and meaningful participation and opportunity in society.
This one also addresses key concepts such as disaggregated data, gender perspectives, socio-economic influences and power-dynamics.

#### What will you learn?
- Mainstream gender, inclusivity and disability inclusion intro training design and delivery
- Apply the inclusivity lenses across the training cycle, from analysis to evaluation
- Integrate inclusive best practices into learning environments, materials and facilitation approaches
- Apply do-no-harm principles to avoid unintended negative consequences in training design and implementation

#### [ITC ILO: Disability in the Workplace](https://www.itcilo.org/courses/disability-workplace) (for disability strategy, HR and talent pipeline)
A bit different from the others, but equally important in theme. This self-paced course by the International Labour Organization (ILO) focuses on building, implementing and evaluating a disability inclusion strategy for your workforce.
I would particularly recommend it as a starting point for professionals in HR, talent pipeline or company culture.
It includes a ten questions pre-knowledge assessment. Like the UNITAR course above, it is pretty short.
